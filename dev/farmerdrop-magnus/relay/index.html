DropRelay

Local food, carried by local businesses.

Meet the Relay Partner, an independent business with its own truck. In this proposed service, partners carry food between farmers, neighborhood HostDrops and members.

Try a sample runCompare route prices

For the sender

State what’s moving, where it needs to go and when. Choose a partner’s quote for that leg. A HostDrop can arrange a second leg to a member’s door.

For the Relay Partner

Choose loads that fit your truck, cold-storage capacity and schedule. Offer your price and pickup time, then carry the food to the person receiving it.

Try a sample run

Take turns as the sender, the Relay Partner and the recipient. All loads, businesses and prices here are examples. Nothing is booked or charged.

Two farms, one HostDrop

Both farms have greens ready on the same morning. A partner with room for both chilled loads could collect from North Ridge, stop at Alder Creek and unload once at Mill Street HostDrop.

Compare the local routes

North Ridge to Mill Street 16 miles
Alder Creek to Mill Street 12 miles

Two trucks deliver the same loads in 28 loaded miles, with two arrivals at the HostDrop.

At an example $3 per loaded mile, the two runs total $84.

North Ridge to Alder Creek 6 miles
Alder Creek to Mill Street 12 miles

One truck delivers both loads in 18 loaded miles, saving 10 miles and one arrival in this example.

At an example $3 per loaded mile, the shared run totals $54.

What would the farmer pay?

A second idea is 10% of product value. North Ridge’s $320 load would cost $32 to carry. Alder Creek’s $480 load would cost $48. Together that’s $80 under either route plan.

These examples compare pricing models. The pilot still needs a rule for who pays each leg and how farmers split a shared run.

Pallet freight gets its own quote. Try the South Florida to Texas lane above to see a partner price the whole haul against the pallet count, equipment and delivery window.
