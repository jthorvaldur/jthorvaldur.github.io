Start a HostDrop in your neighborhood

A HostDrop is a pickup spot at your home, church hall or community room. Neighbors order food online from local farms, the farmers bring it to you, and everyone collects in one stop. This page takes you from "what is this?" to your first pickup day. You do not need any farming, food-business or tech experience.

Draft for review. FarmerDrop's pickup spots are in the Austin, Texas area today. You would be among the first in New Jersey or New York, so some things are not settled yet. Amber boxes say exactly what. Nothing on this page is legal, tax or food safety advice.

The basics

What you do

  • Offer a clean, safe spot and the same pickup time every week.
  • Invite neighbors and approve the ones who ask to join.
  • Count what each farmer delivers and note anything missing or damaged.
  • Hand each neighbor the order they already paid for, and tap "Hand over" in the app.

What you do not do

  • You do not buy food, keep stock or set prices.
  • You do not take money. Neighbors pay online when they order.
  • You do not work out sales tax. The checkout does that from your pickup address.
  • You do not pay to join. FarmerDrop's site says "Join, always free!"

What you earn

  • 10% of the food value of every order you hand over, less a small share of card costs.
  • Example: on a $50.00 order with no sales tax you keep $4.84.
  • The farmer gets 80% and FarmerDrop 10%.
  • Nobody can promise you an income. It depends on how many neighbors order and how often.
Not decided yet

When you get paid. Your earnings show in the app as soon as you record a handover. FarmerDrop sends the money to your bank as a separate step and has not published a schedule.

4

Find your first 15 to 20 households

A farmer will only drive to you if enough people order. Fifteen to twenty friendly households is the size FarmerDrop's first sites are starting with. A regular HostDrop has at least 50.

Before you ask anyone to join, ask them three things and write the answers down: What would you actually buy? How often? Can you collect at this place in this window?

Full detail: Guide 3, Gather your members. It has invitation wording, and the rules: no promises of savings, no mass messages, keep your neighbors' details private.

5

Find your first farmer

You need one farm that grows something your neighbors asked for and is willing to deliver. In the app, a farmer can only connect to pickup spots within 60 miles of the farm by road.

Rule

A list of farms is research. It is not permission. FarmerDrop keeps a research list of farms from public sources. No farm on it has agreed to hear from FarmerDrop or to have its details passed around. Do not write to or call a farm "from FarmerDrop" because you found its name.

What you can do, as yourself:

Words you can use at a market stallWe're organizing a weekly pickup in [neighborhood]. People have asked for [food]. What would make a delivery worthwhile for you? May [name] follow up about [date]?

6

Agree one real offer and one date

Neighbors say yes to something specific: these foods, this price, this day. Agree it with the farmer before you announce anything.

Invite your neighbors to order

Run the readiness check

Do this before the cutoff and again the day before pickup. If any line is unticked, move the date. A late first pickup is better than a failed one.

Full detail: Guide 2, "Readiness check".

Pickup day

Three separate jobs, in this order: receive, get orders ready, hand over. The app records each one, and the records are how the farmer and you get paid.

The morning after, and growing

The second pickup matters more than the first. A neighbor who comes back is a member.

Farms near you

FarmerDrop's research list holds 635 food producers in New Jersey and 4,159 in New York, plus 2,305 in Pennsylvania, 220 in Connecticut and 89 in Delaware, all gathered from public lists. Pick your county to see how many are on file around you. A farm in a neighboring state can deliver to you.

Read these numbers carefully. They count listings, not farms that want to sell to you. Many are wholesale growers, certified organic operations or school suppliers. Distances are straight lines from the middle of the county, so they cross rivers and Long Island Sound; the app's limit is 60 miles by road, which is shorter. Most locations are placed by ZIP code area, not an exact address. 1,501 New York producers and 133 New Jersey producers have no usable location and are not counted here. Rules to check before you start

These notes come from official state pages read on November 25, 2026. They are a starting point for your own phone calls, not legal advice. Rules change, and your county or town may add its own. Confirm each point yourself with the office named.

The question nobody has answered yet

Does a pickup spot like yours count as a "retail food establishment" in New Jersey? We do not know, and this page does not decide it. New Jersey's food code is enforced by local health departments, so yours is the office to ask. Use the wording in step 2.

TopicWhat the official page saysConfirm with
Who to call first The Department of Health's Retail Food page says its project "maintains N.J.A.C. 8:24, Sanitation in Retail Food Establishments, Food and Beverage Machines, and Cottage Food Operations", and sends restaurants, temporary food events ("e.g. town days, carnival, farmers market") and similar businesses to "Your Local Health Department".

NJ Department of Health, Retail Food. Page says last reviewed 9/16/2026.

 Your local health department. Find it with the map on the state's local public health page.
How the food code defines a retail food establishment An older copy of the code on the state's site defines it as "an operation that stores, prepares, packages, serves, vends, or otherwise provides food for human consumption" and "That relinquishes possession of food to a consumer directly, or indirectly through a delivery service". It excludes "A produce stand that only offers whole, uncut fresh fruits and vegetables". We could not read the current text, which the state links to on a legal database.

N.J.A.C. 8:24, copy effective January 2, 2007.

 Your local health department. Ask them to tell you how the current wording applies to you.
Home-baked and home-made food Someone who makes and sells food to consumers must "Have a Cottage Food Operator Permit" or "Comply with applicable laws to retail food establishments". Sales are capped at $50,000 a year. The FAQ says a permit holder "may advertise your business online, accept online orders, and process online payments" but "may not send products to your customers through the mail". The rule lists where the food may be handed over (the maker's home, the customer's New Jersey home, a New Jersey farmers' market or farm stand, a temporary retail food establishment) and bars handing it "to a person who is not the consumer".

Cottage Food Operator Rules and FAQ, NJ Department of Health. No date printed.

 If a home baker wants to sell through your pickup spot, they should ask the Department of Health's cottage food office whether handing orders to you is allowed. Do not assume it is.
Raw (unpasteurized) milk "The sale of raw fluid milk products and raw milk products, except for properly raw aged cheeses, to the ultimate consumer is prohibited."

NJ Department of Health, Starting Up a Dairy in New Jersey. Page says last reviewed 3/24/2023.

 Do not accept raw milk at a New Jersey pickup spot.
What farms may sell direct State guidance for farmers' markets says "fresh, uncut fruits and vegetables can be sold at all farmers markets without restriction". Eggs, meat, poultry, cheese and prepared foods come with source, labelling and cold-storage conditions. These documents are undated and older than the cottage food rule, and two of them disagree on a detail about eggs.

Chapter 24 and You and Minimum Food Safety Requirements for Product Sales, NJ Department of Agriculture with the Department of Health.

 The farmer is responsible for their own licences. Ask each farmer what they hold for what they sell.
Sales tax on food "Most items of food and drink purchased in a food store (supermarket, grocery store, produce market, bakery, etc.) are not subject to Sales Tax." "Sales of prepared food are subject to Sales Tax." The bulletin prints a rate of 6.625% and excludes candy and soft drinks from the exemption. It is dated July 2022 and we did not confirm it is the current edition.

NJ Division of Taxation, Tax Topic Bulletin S&U-4.

 Nothing for you to do: the checkout works out tax. See "Not settled yet" for FarmerDrop's part.
Running something from your home "Local planning offices may have zoning regulations that can affect the type of businesses you can run from home." "Speak to your local office to understand if your business is allowed in your home or building." The cottage food FAQ adds: "Each local municipality has its own zoning laws."

Business.NJ.gov, Select a Location.

 Your town's zoning or planning office.

Not read: the current text of N.J.A.C. 8:24, any county or town health department page, and any local business licence rules.

The question nobody has answered yet

Does a pickup spot like yours need a licence in New York, and from whom? We do not know, and this page does not decide it. New York splits food oversight between the Department of Agriculture and Markets (food stores, processors, warehouses) and health departments (food service). Ask both, using the wording in step 2.

TopicWhat the official page saysConfirm with
Who licenses what The retail food store licence "applies to any retail food store within New York where food and food products are offered to the consumer and intended for off-premises consumption". A warehouse licence applies to "any food warehouse facility within New York in which food is held for commercial distribution". For food service, "contact your local health department".

NY Department of Agriculture and Markets, Food Business Licensing. No date printed.

 The Department of Agriculture and Markets, and your county health department.
How the law defines a retail food store "'Retail food store' means any establishment or section of an establishment where food and food products are offered to the consumer and intended for off-premises consumption." It "does not include establishments which handle only pre-packaged, non-potentially hazardous foods, roadside markets that offer only fresh fruits and fresh vegetables for sale, food service establishments, or food and beverage vending machines."

Agriculture and Markets Law, section 500-a, on the State Senate's site.

 The Department of Agriculture and Markets. Ask them how this applies to you.
Home-baked and home-made food A registered Home Processor may sell approved shelf-stable foods such as breads, cookies and high-acid fruit jams. "All items must be sold within New York State and must be pre-packaged in the home and properly labeled." "Internet sales are allowed within New York State only." "Any finished food product that requires refrigeration is prohibited". The page lists home delivery and the internet among the ways to sell.

NY Department of Agriculture and Markets, Home Processing. No date printed.

 A home baker who wants to sell through your pickup spot should confirm with the Department that handing orders to you is covered.
Raw (unpasteurized) milk "To sell raw cow milk directly from the farm to consumers, a producer is required to obtain a Raw Milk Sales Part 2 permit." The regulation the Department links to says a permit holder may sell raw milk only "directly to a consumer" and "on the dairy farm where such raw milk is produced".

NY Department of Agriculture and Markets, Raw Milk and its raw milk regulations. No date printed.

 Do not accept raw milk at a New York pickup spot.
What farms may sell direct "A license is not required to sell fruits or vegetables that have been grown and harvested." Beef, pork, goat and sheep "must be slaughtered and processed at a USDA inspected processing facility and bear a USDA mark of inspection". "Eggs must be stored with an ambient temperature of 45°F or less." Baked goods must come from a licensed facility, a registered Home Processor or a health-permitted kitchen. The document is written for farmers' markets and carries no date.

Guidance on Sanitary Regulations for New York State Farmers Markets, NY Department of Agriculture and Markets.

 The farmer is responsible for their own licences. Ask each farmer what they hold. If you store eggs or meat, ask how cold they must be kept.
Sales tax on food "Generally, food and food products sold by food stores are exempt from sales tax. However, there are exceptions." Food is taxable when "it is sold heated", sold to eat on the premises, or prepared by the seller and ready to eat. Sandwiches, carbonated drinks, candy and pet food are listed as taxable.

NY Department of Taxation and Finance, Tax Bulletin ST-283, issued April 8, 2019.

 Nothing for you to do: the checkout works out tax. See "Not settled yet" for FarmerDrop's part.
Running something from your home "Be sure to consult with your local zoning officials for approval before commencing any home-based business." The state's business site adds: "Many counties, cities, towns, and villages also require local permits and licenses."

Home Processing page and NY Business Express, How to Start a Business in New York.

 Your town, village or city clerk and zoning office.

Not read: the state Department of Health's directory of county health departments (its site refused our requests), any county health department's rules, and the sales tax guidance for farm stands. Search for your county's health department by name.

Words you'll hear

HostDrop
Your pickup spot, and the page for it in the app.
Host
You.
Member
A neighbor who has joined your HostDrop and can order.
Farmer
The farm or food producer who lists food and delivers it to you.
DropDate
The pickup date. Everything in the app is organized by it.
Cutoff
The moment ordering closes for a DropDate: 12 noon, a set number of days before delivery. Each farmer chooses the number of days.
Receiving
The screen where you count what a farmer delivered. Saving it confirms the farmer's earning.
Handover
Giving a member their order and tapping Hand over. This confirms your earning.
Ledger
The screen that shows what you have earned and what has been paid out.
Payment Setup
Connecting your bank account so FarmerDrop can pay you. It is done through Stripe, a payments company.
DropChat
Messages inside the app between you, your farmers and your members.
